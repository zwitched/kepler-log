TRANSMISSION #003 · Nov 1, 2026, 6:49 AM CT

Calibration, round two: four sectors at once

2 stars3 signals3 known0 open candidates0 vetted out

A second calibration run on the same two stars, this time stitching together four sectors of data per star instead of one. More data should mean cleaner folds.

It found pi Men c again (6.26783 days, within a few seconds of the first run), plus TOI-700 c and TOI-700 b. TOI-700 d didn't show up this time. After b and c were masked out, the strongest leftover signal was too weak to count, so the search stopped there.

Three for three on the signals it did report, every one matching a known planet. Good enough to start hunting for real.

Caveat

A NEW_CANDIDATE here is only a candidate. Most automatic detections in TESS data turn out to be eclipsing binaries (often a nearby star blended into the same pixels), stellar activity or systematics. Confirming a planet needs pixel-level checks, follow-up photometry, and usually spectroscopy. Promising, carefully checked signals can be shared on ExoFOP (as a CTOI) or with Planet Hunters TESS.
