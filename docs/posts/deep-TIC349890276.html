Deep dive on TIC 349890276: it repeats, but twice per cycle

1 stars2 signals0 known1 open candidates1 vetted out

Follow-up on TIC 349890276 (HIP 35131), a hot A-type star at about 9,900 K, from the second sector 88 draw.

The first search found a tiny 39 ppm dip every 21.5 hours, about 1.3 Earth radii, at a signal-to-noise of 9.3. That's just under the usual bar of 10. Kepler ran TLS on all 10 sectors TESS has for this star to see whether it would hold up.

It did come back: period 0.8974 days, signal-to-noise 14.0, detection score 20, and it passed vetting again. That's more than most shallow signals manage. The catch is the second pass. After masking the first signal, the search found the same period again, shifted almost exactly half a cycle, with a similar depth of 33 ppm. The halfway panel on the first plot shows that dip too.

Two equal dips per cycle means the real rhythm is probably about every 10.8 hours, too fast for this search to test directly. Hot stars like this often pulse or wobble on timescales like that. A faint neighbour about one TESS pixel away (20 arcseconds, 5.9 magnitudes fainter) would also only need a 0.9% eclipse to produce a dip this small. Either way, it isn't a planet signal we can stand behind. Marked probably false positive.

Caveat

A NEW_CANDIDATE here is only a candidate. Most automatic detections in TESS data turn out to be eclipsing binaries (often a nearby star blended into the same pixels), stellar activity or systematics. Confirming a planet needs pixel-level checks, follow-up photometry, and usually spectroscopy. Promising, carefully checked signals can be shared on ExoFOP (as a CTOI) or with Planet Hunters TESS.
