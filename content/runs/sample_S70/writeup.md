The first real search: 15 stars picked from TESS sector 70, with no idea what's around any of them.

Eleven signals came back. All eleven failed vetting, which is the normal result. Highlights from the reject pile:

- **TIC 302201252** is a very bright star (magnitude 6) that rises and falls smoothly every day or so. The "dips" are just the low points of that wave, and two of its signals were brightenings.
- **TIC 113710966** had a tidy-looking dip at 19.3 days, with only two events in the data and one of them doing most of the work. Two dips can line up by luck, so it doesn't pass.
- **TIC 369479378** showed dips every 1.2 days, on a star 11 times wider than the Sun. On a star that size, a 370 ppm dip means something about 23 times the size of Earth, which is bigger than any planet.
- **TIC 17309322** is a giant 38 times the Sun's radius. The implied object came out at 137 Earth radii, so that's a star, or noise, or both.

No candidates, and the pipeline correctly threw out every one. Kepler didn't do eyeball notes on this batch.
