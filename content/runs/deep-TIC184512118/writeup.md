Follow-up on TIC 184512118 (HIP 41656), from the third sector 88 draw.

The first search found a tiny 39 ppm dip every 1.1234 days (about 27 hours), with a signal-to-noise of 10.6, right on the bar. TLS on all seven sectors TESS has for this star found the same period again, 1.123448 days across 141 transits, with a signal-to-noise of 10.2.

That's the good news. The bad news is a 10.7 ppm dip exactly halfway between the main ones, detected at 3.2σ, which tripped the secondary-eclipse check. A second, shallower eclipse at the halfway point is what two stars orbiting each other look like.

The field makes it worse. This star sits among dozens of fainter ones. TIC 184512108, 12.6 arcseconds away and 5.8 magnitudes fainter, would only need to dim by 0.8% to produce the whole signal, and another neighbour at 48 arcseconds would need just 0.5%. The TIC catalogue also has no temperature or radius for this star, so there's no size estimate. Marked *probably false positive*. The second TLS signal, at 19.5 days, failed on odd/even depths and rested mostly on one event.
