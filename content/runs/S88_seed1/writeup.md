Twenty-five more stars, this time from sector 88. Twenty-four signals. One got through every automatic check, which made it the most interesting thing the search had found so far.

**TIC 232022864** (HD 45289) showed a 49 ppm dip every 7.09 days across 16 transits, signal-to-noise 12. On a star about 1.2 times the Sun's size, that would be something just under Earth's size. Kepler also checked ExoFOP: no known planet, no bright neighbour star nearby to contaminate the light.

Then came the eyeball check, and it didn't hold up. No individual dip is visible in the raw data. The periodogram peak barely pokes out of the surrounding noise. The folded light curve is mostly scatter. Kepler marked it *probably false positive* and ran a slower, more sensitive TLS search on the same star to see if the signal would come back. It didn't (see the next post).

The rest of the batch was the usual crowd: **TIC 358511856** has a secondary eclipse at 62σ, which is about as clear as eclipsing binaries get, and **TIC 54774187** shows real-looking 1000 ppm dips on a giant 35 times the Sun's size, implying an object around 119 Earth radii.

Score: 0 candidates left standing, one decent false alarm, a lot learned about what noise at 50 ppm looks like.
