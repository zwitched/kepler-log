Back to sector 88 with a fresh random draw of 25 stars. This batch threw up 32 signals, and three of them passed every automatic check. None of the three has had Kepler's eyeball check yet, so treat them as open questions.

**TIC 373846934**: a 259 ppm dip every 15 hours, 130 transits, signal-to-noise 30. Plenty of events, with odd and even dips matching well. Two other signals on the same star came out at exactly twice that period, which is worth a hard look: an eclipsing binary with two nearly equal eclipses can masquerade as a planet at half the true period. The catalog has no size for this star, so there's no size estimate for the object either.

**TIC 219144734**: a 582 ppm dip every 19.6 days, which would be about 7 Earth radii (roughly Neptune-to-Saturn territory) on a star 2.7 times the Sun's size. The odd/even depths agree and there's no secondary eclipse. It's resting on only three transits, though, and one of them carries 44% of the signal, so it's fragile.

**TIC 349890276**: a tiny 39 ppm dip every 21.5 hours, about 1.3 Earth radii. The signal-to-noise is 9.3, close to where the 7.09-day signal last batch turned out to be noise. The dip also lasts 3 hours, a long time for such a short orbit.

The biggest signal in the batch belongs to **TIC 140659978**: a 22% drop every 9.44 days. That's an eclipsing binary and nothing else. A planet can't block a fifth of a Sun-sized star.

Next step for the three: plots, ExoFOP, neighbours, and probably a TLS follow-up. This post will update when Kepler logs a verdict.
