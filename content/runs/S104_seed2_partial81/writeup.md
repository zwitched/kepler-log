This is a **partial** look at the second Sector 104 draw, not the finished 200-star run. Nick asked to check whatever was done rather than wait. At freeze time, **81 of 200** stars had finished (53 with signals, 28 quiet, zero hard errors). Those 81 are locked in `S104_seed2_partial81` so the still-running hunt cannot rewrite them. The live job keeps going toward 200 in the original folder; nothing here claims that target is done.

Of the 81, three stars had already been searched in the first Sector 104 draw (seed 1). Seventy-eight are new. The pipeline returned 129 signals. Six passed every automatic check. Five of those six fall apart under a closer look. One is still open, weakly.

**TIC 100895652** is the provisional survivor. Every 8.74 days the star dims by about 420 ppm for four hours, five times across sectors 27, 104 and 105. The dip shows up in both the cleaned and the raw light curves, the odd and even dips roughly match, and nothing obvious sits at the halfway point. If it were a planet it would be about 3.2 Earth radii around a 1.4-solar-radius F star. Reasons to doubt it: only five dips, the detection statistic is barely above the cut, and the same star already failed two other automatic signals at about 1.3 days with a clear variable-star shape. A neighbour 14 arcseconds away is bright enough that a deep eclipse there could leak into these pixels; that neighbour's light curve has not been checked yet, and a full-sector deep search is still waiting because the live 200-star run is using the archive. Marked *unsure*.

The other five automatic passes are probably false positives. **TIC 160188533** looked like a tidy 0.95-day, 310 ppm signal until Gaia reported RUWE 13.2 and a non-single-star flag, and the raw flux depth collapsed from 370 ppm in one sector to 36 ppm in the next. **TIC 111363724** is a giant six times the Sun's size; a central transit should last about 16 hours, but the signal is only three hours long, which would need an almost impossible graze. **TIC 406244401** is the clearest instrument story of the bunch: the cleaned light curve shows a dip while the raw one brightens at the same phase, and the depth jumps around from sector to sector. **TIC 165124046** has mismatched odd and even depths on a Gaia-flagged variable star. **TIC 281765034** has a 12-day period but only a 45-minute dip, far too short for its star, with depths that wander by sector.

Score for this freeze: 81 stars checked, 129 signals, six automatic passes, five ruled out, one weak open candidate. The other 119 stars of the 200-star draw are still in progress and will get their own write-up when that run actually finishes.
